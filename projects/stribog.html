Stribog - CO2 sensor

To avoid poor sleep or worse decision making and focus, the Slavic god of wind, sky and air tells me when CO2 reaches a critical concentration and pushes my to open my windows.

Context

The idea began during the COVID era and then was accelerated with the discovery of Birdie (the CO2 monitoring station) and reading about the effect of CO2 concentration on sleep quality, focus and decision-making ability. However, between the price for Birdie and the curiosity to understand and create a device that could tell me when to open the windows to stay alert, I started looking into creating such device and I chose to build it using Home Assistant as the backbone. Before trying to build one I looked for already existing devices, but the cheap ones are not really good or need monitoring to check if a threshold is reached (and I don't want a strong buzzing during the night!). The others are quite expensive (hello Birdie) even though its design and additional motor can justify an increased price. Moreover, these devices are not modular and I can not tinker with them to automatize anything easily, that's where I started looking to build one.

The CO2 concentration is measured in [ppm] - part per million, and generally we can admit that there are typical levels to observe when looking at air quality. Below 800 ppm, the air is quite good and the concentration in carbon dioxide should not alter judgement/reflexion speed (note that the average concentration outside sits around 400 to 500ppm in the countryside or the city). Between 800 and 1200 ppm, there can be slight drops in attention and concentration, and above 1200 ppm it's clearly time to open the window and let air circulate inside.

Concentration [ppm]Condition and effects
400-800Good conditions
800-1200Deteriorating conditions, slight lost in focus and dizziness
>1200Poor conditions, sleepiness can begin time to open the windows !

There are already many different tutorials on how to build a CO2 sensor with Home Assistant, but there is something that always bothers me, you need to constantly keep the captor plugged in, even if you only take a measure every five minutes. Moreover, it takes an electric plug spot and a when you have small rooms with limited power plugs, you're quickly out of options. You could say, I plug it to my computer, but as I am usually using a laptop, I don't want to have it using on of the USB plugs. These different reasons explain why I aim to build a battery powered sensor, taking advantage of the deep sleep mode of the ESP32C3 (more about that in the V2).

Objective

Create a battery powered CO2 sensor communicating with the home server to launch an alert when the concentration reaches 1200 ppm.

Realization

The project is built around 2 mains components, the sensor, an SCD41 from Sensirion (more info here) and the brain, an ESP32C3 from SeeedStudio (bought un soldered in pack of 3 - here). The building of the final sensor is made through 3 iterations:

  • V1 is not yet battery powered, the juice is provided by the USB-C connection of the ESP32C3,
  • V2 is created to test the battery powering of the ESP and sensor
  • V3 goes a step further and aim to create a complete device, with PCB and 3D printed case
